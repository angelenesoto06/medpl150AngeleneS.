Reading Reflection 1 (9/11)

When I take photos, I try to capture special memories. For instance, if I am having fun with friends I try to take a photo or video. That way I can always look at that photo and smile. My favorite photos to take were of my cat, Silley. She could be standing still or jumping around and I would snap a photo right then and there. I used to use my mother’s iPad to edit some photos that I took of her. I would make them darker, lighter. I would blur the background of the photos just to make her the main focus of the photos. I think those photos represented how much she meant to me. How I could forget about all my troubles or anything else I was going through the moment she looked at me. By making her the center of those photos and blurring the background, I feel that viewers looking at those photos could tell how much she meant to me and the effect she had on me. I also love taking photos of my brothers when they are walking down the street. I have plenty of photos of my brothers walking and they range from when my brothers first started walking to now. I feel like these photos are metaphoric. As not only are my brothers walking down a path, these photos show them walking through life, growing and changing with each step they take. It may sound cliché but looking through those makes me so proud of my brothers. How far they have come, how much they have grown. I feel that all my photos symbolize something important in my life, if you look deep enough. I feel that the best way to make connections with our world through photography is to combine images of many important times. For example, If I wanted to spread a message about gender inequality, my first thought would be to create collaborative photos of Alice Paul and other Feminist protest events. Alice Paul was a suffragist who advocated for women’s rights. She was arrested after holding a protest to campaign so women would receive the right to vote. In jail, she was treated terribly and force fed after going on a hunger strike. She is a true inspiration and because of the work her and others did, we have the right to vote. So If I wanted to spread a message about equality, I would make a collage of photos of her. She stood up against a norm in her society and brought change even though she struggled because of it. Paul is proof that If you want something you have to fight for it and even though you may struggle, you may just start a revolution. To me, this is what photos should do. They should tell a story that allows people to connect/relate to the photo. Everyone can relate to having something or someone in their life who helps them through hard events, and everyone can relate to watching someone they love grow up or breaking the status quo.

Homework 4 Reflection

When AI first started becoming widely read, I remember being excited and believed it was downright genius. However, now AI scares me a bit. AI has gotten so advanced that it’s hard to tell what’s real and what is fake. I can tell if a video is AI or not because typically, the background of those videos can get a bit blurry. However, AI has advanced so far that pretty soon we will not be able to tell at all. This scares me because it can lead to false messages being spread or hurtful ones. There was a “Trend” on Tiktok going around not so long ago in which people would make AI videos of people who had passed, Like Michael Jackson, Martin Luther King Jr, and many more. I found these videos so disrespectful because imagine the families of these people seeing these videos. Seeing your loved one in a video in which they do something so ridiculous like stealing people’s food at a fast food restaurant (which is what some of the videos looked like) is so disrespectful because it makes them look silly and makes their legacies look like one big joke. Not to mention, many of these celebrities cannot consent to these videos being made, nor can they do anything to stop it. People may think it’s just a joke, but it can deeply affect the families of these people, and I feel that is not taken into consideration.Additionally, AI can spread false messages to many people. I have many older family members who have seen AI videos and cannot tell if it's AI. A lot of my elderly family members do not know about AI or understand its capabilities. I get so many messages from family members sending me outrageous news stories that are created by AI just to get views. Similarly, I remember when the 2024 elections were happening, someone created an AI version of Taylor Swift in which she said she stood by Donald Trump and said she was voting for him. I do not know who created the AI but a lot of people were shocked by this statement. Taylor Swift then addressed the statement saying that it was completely False, and that she was voting for Kamala Harris. This AI creation was obviously political propaganda, but it is even more upsetting when you think about it. Taylor Swift is one of the top stars in our generation right now, and her fan base is very young. Spreading messages like these may cause people to believe political messages that are not true. Taylor Swift serves as a role model for some people and many people like to follow her lead. Imagine the disappointment of some people when they realize that they have been lied to. Or the disappointment of many others who have been played because of messages similar to these. AI is a very powerful tool that we have. I do not think we need to get rid of it or stop using it completely. I think there has to be limits on AI because of all the capabilities it has. AI can do so much, so we should be responsible and use it for better purposes.

Homework 6 Reflection

Homework 6 Reflection: I read the story Heela: The Schoolgirl, My stolen future. I deeply enjoyed the story. I have read stories like Heela’s before, however one thing that stood out to me was how by using the images, we were able to be in Heela’s shoes. The illustrations really helped me understand how she was feeling at certain moments. In the story, Heela finds out that she is pregnant, After finding out that she is pregnant, the story flips to the next panel and we see Heela, who looks emotional, as if she is about to cry, talking to her friend. At this moment, Heela seems to be panicked and a bit confused, As I would imagine anyone would be in her situation. The difference is that usually in these stories, the author would usually write something to the effect of this person is scared, etc. So to see someone's reaction and how they felt at that exact moment, makes it a lot more meaningful because I was able to see her fear and confusion about her situation. Additionally, when Heela’s grandmother finds her later on in the story, Heela is sitting under a tree in a cradle position, seemingly rocking and hugging herself. I found this detail interesting because there is some psychology to it. I have heard when humans go into a cradle-like position, it is our way of comforting ourselves. I do not think that this detail would have been covered in a book. But I appreciate this detail because as I have mentioned before, Heela fears what will become of her life due to her pregnancy, and her hugging herself really helps understand how deep that fear is. Overall, the drawings include details that may seem minor, but can actually add a lot to the story if you really look at them, and it can really deepen a story. Additionally, In the story, everything was drawn, However the story included only 3 images of this woman named Neneh Conteh, who is a counsellor, at the Rainbo Centre that Heela stood at shortly after she found out she was pregnant. I believe this image was included because Neneh Conteh had a great impact on Heela. In the story Heela mentions that when she was transported to the Rainbo Centre, she finally felt safe. Heela was assaulted and thus, that is why she became pregnant. The Rainbo Centre offered her counseling to help her deal with her assault and also provided materials for her to help her during her pregnancy. I believe that this woman is the only thing that is now drawn so she can stand out. By having Neneh Conteh as a picture and not a drawing, I believe it represents how important Neneh Conteh is to Heela, and how much her help and support really guided Neneh Conteh through this difficult time. There is also a video close to the end of the story in which she explains that now people report cases like Heela’s because they feel safe to do so. I believe that Neneh Conteh and the Rainbo Centre represent hope for many young women who have experienced similar things to Heela, and sends a message that even if a situation is dark, there is light at the end of the tunnel and you are not alone.
